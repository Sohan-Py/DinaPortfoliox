01 // ABOUT ME | ENGINEERING PROFILE

Building Mechanical Systems From Component to Motion

INSTITUTION: JADAVPUR UNIVERSITY  •  DEPT: MECHANICAL ENG.
SPECIFICATION // BIOGRAPHY REF: JU-MECH-UG

I am a pre-final year Mechanical Engineering student at Jadavpur University with a deep focus on 3D CAD modeling, complex mechanical assembly, and mechanism design. My work revolves around understanding how individual mechanical components come together to form functional, moving machines.

Rather than treating CAD as purely static geometry, I approach every project as a complete mechanical system — modeling each shaft, housing, gear, blade, and linkage from the ground up, applying accurate geometric and kinematic assembly constraints, and studying how forces and motion propagate through the assembly.

My portfolio includes high-part-count engineering systems such as multi-stage jet engine assemblies, reciprocating piston-engine mechanisms, compound gear trains, and articulated robotic mechanisms — built and analyzed across PTC Creo Parametric, Autodesk Fusion 360, and SolidWorks.

PROPULSION & ROTATING MACHINERY

Jet Engine Assembly

A complete multi-stage axial-flow jet engine modeled from individual rotor and stator components in PTC Creo Parametric, featuring a sectioned outer casing to visualize internal compression, combustion, and turbine stages in synchronized motion.

// Key Engineering & Assembly Highlights

  • 01 Modeled individual intake fan blades, multi-stage compressor rotors, stationary stator vane rings, annular combustion liner, and turbine stages.
  • 02 Applied coaxial, planar, and pin assembly constraints along the central drive shaft to unify high- and low-pressure rotor spools.
  • 03 Engineered a longitudinal cutaway section of the nacelle and outer casing to expose internal blade tip clearances and flow passages.
  • 04 Simulated continuous 360° rotational kinematics to verify concentric rotor spin relative to fixed stator stages without geometric clash.
  • 05 Produced multi-angle orthographic, cutaway, and dynamic motion captures demonstrating full mechanical integration.

RECIPROCATING MECHANISM & KINEMATICS

Piston Engine Assembly

A fully constrained internal combustion piston-crankshaft mechanism modeled in PTC Creo Parametric, demonstrating accurate linear-to-rotary motion conversion through pin and cylinder kinematic joints.

// Key Engineering & Assembly Highlights

  • 01 Modeled piston crowns with ring grooves, gudgeon (wrist) pins, H-beam connecting rods, split bearing caps, and a counterweighted crankshaft.
  • 02 Defined slider/cylinder constraints along the cylinder bore axis and revolute pin joints at the crankpin and wrist pin interfaces.
  • 03 Evaluated Top Dead Center (TDC) and Bottom Dead Center (BDC) stroke geometry, connecting rod swing clearance, and counterweight balance.
  • 04 Integrated big-end bolts, journal bearings, and retainer clips with zero part interference across the full 360° crank cycle.
  • 05 Captured real-time kinematic motion studies and multi-angle assembly views for technical presentation.
